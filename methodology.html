X1

Attack risk

normalized 0–1 · hromada-level
Description

Intensity of missile, drone, and artillery activity affecting the hromada specifically — disaggregated down from oblast level.

Data sources

ACLED Ukraine event data (registered-account export, weekly, oblast/admin1-level) — event counts for Air/drone strike and Shelling/artillery/missile attack sub-types.

Kaggle — piterfm "massive-missile-attacks-on-ukraine" (national daily weapon-launch detail: Shahed, cruise, ballistic). Reference context only; not yet merged into a per-hromada column. Ukrainian Air Force stopped publishing ballistic-missile figures 2026-08-10; Shahed/cruise reporting continues.

Methodology

ACLED itself only publishes at oblast resolution (confirmed: every event in a given oblast/week shares one centroid coordinate). To get within-oblast spread, each oblast's total is disaggregated across its hromadas by weighted share, split by sub-event type since the two behave differently in space:

shelling_share(h) = exp(−distance_to_frontline_km / 30) ÷ Σ oblast (short-range → concentrates near the front; occupied hromadas treated as distance 0) airdrone_share(h) = population_2022(h) × (0.5 + 0.5 × urban_pct(h)) ÷ Σ oblast (long-range → targets population/urban centers regardless of front-line distance) hromada_attack_events = oblast_shelling_total × shelling_share + oblast_airdrone_total × airdrone_share

Disaggregation is conservative — each oblast's hromada-level values sum back exactly to its original ACLED total, verified by spot-check. This is a modeling assumption layered on real ACLED counts, not a directly sourced hromada-level figure — the true within-oblast pattern could differ.

e.g. Zaporizhzhia oblast: city hromada 6,539 events (pop. 710k) vs Dniprorudne 193 (1.5km from the line, pop. 18k)
Normalization

Min–max normalized across all 1,469 hromadas into attack_risk_score, range 0.000–1.000.

X2

Outage risk

normalized 0–1 composite
Description

Likelihood of heating/power/water disruption this winter, blending strike history, documented energy-infrastructure damage, and front-line exposure.

Data sources

X1's ACLED-derived strike counts (reused).

Hand-compiled facility register (documented_powerplant_damage.csv) — 18 named power plants/substations/dams with status, dates and a source link each (Reuters, NV, Ukrainska Pravda, Wikipedia, DTEK reporting), geolocated to a hromada.

X3's front-line distance / occupation flag (reused).

Methodology
outage_risk_proxy = 0.20 × strike_history_norm + 0.75 × documented_damage_norm + 0.05 × frontline_occupation_flag

Damage term: a hromada hosting a documented facility takes that facility's severity (destroyed=3, damaged=2, at-risk=1) ÷ 3. Only 15 of 1,469 hromadas host one directly, so a hromada with none inherits 40% of its oblast's worst severity — grid damage cascades regionally, and this avoids a flat zero for 99% of hromadas. 845 hromadas get this cascade signal; 609 have none documented anywhere in their oblast.

Front-line flag = 1 if currently occupied or ≤10 km from the front line, else 0.

Normalization

Fully normalized, range 0.000–1.000. Highest: Nova Kakhovka hromada (destroyed dam, occupied, high strike activity).

X3

Border / front-line proximity

normalized 0–1
Description

Distance from the hromada's center to the nearest currently-occupied territory boundary — the most direct proxy for shelling and ground-assault risk.

Data sources

DeepState occupied-territory map (cyterat/deepstate-map-data GitHub mirror, GPL-3.0, daily-updated GeoJSON).

Methodology

Point-to-polygon nearest-boundary distance computed with shapely, converted to km via haversine great-circle formula. Still the only variable computed independently per hromada rather than disaggregated or joined from an oblast average.

range: 0.4–776.6 km
currently occupied: 169 hromadas
as of: 2026-09-09, refreshes with DeepState
Normalization
frontline_proximity_score = 1.0 if currently_occupied exp(−distance_km / 30) otherwise

Same 30km decay scale as X1's shelling component, for consistency — risk falls off sharply within ~30km and is negligible past ~150–200km, which matches real artillery/rocket exposure patterns better than a linear scale would. Occupied hromadas are pinned to maximum risk outright, since "distance to the boundary" is ambiguous once you're inside it.

X4

Logistics risk

oblast-level proxy · not normalized
Description

Difficulty of moving people, fuel, and supplies in/out — retail/food-logistics and transport-infrastructure exposure.

Data sources

Carried over from Marie's prior "Winter Ukraine Displace Safe" analysis (logistics_risk_dataset.csv, from her build_dataset.py) — a hand-assigned 0–1 score per oblast built from two August 2026 news reports on warehouse strikes (Euromaidan Press, United24 Media), not a systematic logistics-infrastructure census.

Methodology

Joined onto every hromada by oblast, unchanged from the source table — no within-oblast disaggregation attempted here, since the rationale itself is mostly oblast-wide ("regional retail-distribution exposure") rather than tied to one location. One row (Kyiv oblast) does name a specific site — RLC-Kvitnev in Brovary — which could be pinned to its own hromada later the way X2's facilities were, if useful.

Important asymmetry Marie flagged: 14 of 22 assessed oblasts score exactly 0.00, meaning "no source documented comparable infrastructure there" — not "confirmed low risk." Donetsk and Luhansk aren't in the source table at all and are kept as unassessed (blank), not zero, to preserve that distinction.

highest: Kyiv oblast 1.00 (Brovary warehouse strikes)
lowest nonzero: Lviv 0.15 (relocated hub, risk-mitigating)
22 of 24 oblasts assessed · 2 unassessed (Donetsk, Luhansk)
Normalization

Already on a 0–1 scale by construction, but not min–max normalized against the other variables and not hromada-differentiated — treat it as the weakest-evidenced of the four.

Σ

Overall winter-safety score

normalized 0–1 · equal-weighted
Description

Single per-hromada ranking combining all four variables — this is what drives the "green cluster" comparison between a person's current location and candidate relocation destinations.

Methodology
overall_winter_safety_score = mean(X1, X2, X3, X4) ← equal weight, 25% each

Equal weighting is a default, not a confirmed decision — Marie's prior analysis hasn't specified relative importance across X1–X4 yet, so all four count the same for now. For the 103 hromadas in Donetsk/Luhansk oblasts where X4 is unassessed (not zero), the mean is taken over the 3 available variables instead of dividing by a missing fourth — excluding a genuinely unknown input rather than guessing a number for it.

1,366 hromadas scored on 4/4 variables
103 hromadas scored on 3/4 (X4 unassessed)
Highest risk

Kherson city hromada (0.697), Nova Kakhovka (0.649), Svitlodarsk (0.639), Enerhodar (0.540), Zaporizhzhia city (0.511).

Lowest risk

A cluster of Volyn oblast hromadas near the Polish/Belarusian border score 0.000 across the board — a natural first candidate pool for "green cluster" destinations.

Known gaps & caveats

  • X1's within-oblast spread is modeled, not directly sourced — ACLED itself only publishes oblast totals; the split across hromadas (front-line decay for shelling, population for air/drone) is a defensible proxy, not a fact, and would benefit from validation against any facility- or city-level strike reporting.
  • X2's damage component is sparse by nature — only 18 facilities documented by hand; the 40% oblast-cascade discount is a modeling choice, not a sourced number, and should be sanity-checked or replaced if a facility-level dataset becomes available.
  • Kyiv city is excluded — it has separate administrative status and isn't one of the 1,469 KSE hromadas, so documented damage there (e.g. Kyiv TEC-5) can't be joined in.
  • The combined score's weighting is a placeholder — equal 25% weights until Marie confirms whether some variables should count more than others, the way X2's sub-components were deliberately weighted 20/75/5.
  • X4 is the thinnest variable — a 2-source, hand-assigned oblast score, not a downloaded dataset; its 0.00 values mean "undocumented," not "safe," and Donetsk/Luhansk are unassessed rather than zero.
  • DeepState source is GPL-3.0 — worth revisiting before any commercial or government redistribution of the front-line layer.
